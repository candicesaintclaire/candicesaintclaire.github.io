About

A reseller who can't stop
researching.

I started as a reseller and I still am one. But somewhere along the way, the research became the point — and the things I was finding started demanding more than a price tag.

I apply a consistent research methodology to historically and culturally significant items — one where each step informs the next, and the path adapts based on what the evidence reveals. I have directly verified the rarity of holdings through institutional research, including correspondence with the Library of Congress, the British Library, the National Library of Scotland, and Stanford University's David Rumsey Map Center.

Origin

Where this comes from.

I have always loved old things. I was raised by my great-grandmother, who was born in 1919. She instilled in me a deep and early love of reading — and I tore through books constantly. The ones I loved most were about olden times. History wasn't abstract to me growing up. It had a face, a voice, and it made me breakfast.

That connection to the past has always felt like a foundational part of who I am. The storage unit work gave it a container and a purpose — but the instinct was already there, long before I ever bid on my first unit.

My professional background spans insurance investigation and claims analysis, which trained me to think rigorously about evidence, provenance, and documentation. Combined with my work in software development, I approach archival research as an analyst first: systematic, skeptical, and committed to verifying what I think I know before I say it out loud.

Philosophy

On stewardship and the thrill of the hunt.

The history these objects carry is the whole point — the people who made them, owned them, carried them forward.

Most resellers live and die by the thrill of the hunt. For me, the thrill comes later — as I piece together the story, contextualizing bits and pieces into a coherent narrative that honors the sum of that history.

I think about who held these things before me. Who made them. What role they played in someone's life. And I try to consider where they should go next — not just what they will yield, but what they deserve. The first question is never "how do I obtain the highest dollar amount for this item" on its own. The guiding question is how to please two masters: the financial one, and the one that reigns longer and wider — the historical one.

Thankfully, with truly world-class items, those two masters tend to want the same thing.

Methodology

The SIS Framework — Science, Intuition, Strategy.

SIS is less a rigid framework and more a living methodology. One that starts fresh with every find, and ends the same way every time — with the item moving forward, understood and honored, into whatever comes next.

01
Science

Repeatable processes. Logical thinking. Mental models. Every claim is documented and traceable. Institutional databases, primary sources, and verification through correspondence with libraries and archives. Nothing is assumed.

02
Intuition

Pattern recognition honed over years of investigative work — the ability to sense that something is different before the evidence has fully revealed itself. At its heart, intuition is unrecognized pattern recognition. It doesn't replace rigor; it initiates it.

03
Strategy

Thinking forward to where an item should ultimately land, and backward to keep perspective on what it has already survived. Positioning materials for the right market, the right buyer, and the right moment — on the item's timeline, not just mine.
